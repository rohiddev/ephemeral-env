PART 3 — THE HEADLINE USE CASE: NUDGE TECHNOLOGY
---

"Nudge technology" for call center agents is the specific AI use case
the JD calls out. You need to own this concept completely.

WHAT IT IS
  Real-time AI assistance displayed to a call center representative
  while they are on a live call with a customer.
  The agent does not stop to search a knowledge base.
  The AI listens (or reads the transcript in near-real-time),
  understands the context, and surfaces:
    - Relevant knowledge base articles
    - Suggested next best action
    - Compliance reminders ("you must read this disclosure")
    - Benefit plan details specific to the caller's employer
    - Recommended responses to specific objections
    - Upsell or retention offers based on customer profile
    - Escalation triggers ("this caller sounds at risk of churn")

WHY IT IS CALLED "NUDGE"
  It nudges the human agent toward better decisions rather than
  replacing the agent. The human remains in control.
  The AI reduces cognitive load, reduces average handle time (AHT),
  improves first call resolution (FCR), and reduces errors.

THE ARCHITECTURE
  Real-time call transcript -> ASR (speech-to-text, AWS Transcribe / Google STT)
  Transcript stream -> intent classifier (what topic is the caller asking about?)
  Intent + caller context (CRM data, plan data, history) -> RAG retrieval
  Retrieved knowledge + intent -> LLM generates suggested response / next action
  Suggestion surfaces on agent desktop (Salesforce Service Cloud CTI widget)
  Agent sees suggestion, uses it or ignores it, continues the call
  Interaction logged: what was suggested, was it used, call outcome

  This is your multi-agent architecture applied to a call center context:
  - Intent classifier (your intent_classifier.py)
  - Specialist retrieval agents (your domain specialists via Bedrock KB)
  - Real-time aggregation (your aggregator.py)
  - Delivered as a widget in the agent desktop, not a chat interface

THE METRICS THAT MATTER
  AHT (Average Handle Time): time from call answer to call end.
    Nudge reduces AHT by surfacing answers faster.
    Benchmark: 3-5% AHT reduction is meaningful at call center scale.
    At 1000 calls/day * 5 minutes AHT * 5% reduction = 250 minutes/day saved.

  FCR (First Call Resolution): did the issue get resolved on the first call?
    Nudge improves FCR by giving the agent the right answer on the first call.
    Industry average FCR: 70-75%. Improving to 80% is significant.

  CSAT (Customer Satisfaction Score): post-call survey.
    Better answers, shorter calls, fewer escalations = higher CSAT.

  Compliance adherence: did the agent read required disclosures?
    Nudge surfaces mandatory disclosures as required prompts.
    Reduces regulatory risk. Auditable (was the prompt shown? was it acknowledged?).

  Knowledge base deflection: what % of calls used AI-suggested answers
    vs the agent manually searching. Measures adoption.

HOW TO TALK ABOUT IT IN THE INTERVIEW
  "The nudge technology use case is essentially a real-time supervisor agent
  running alongside the human agent on every call. The call transcript is
  processed continuously. An intent classifier identifies the topic in near
  real-time — is the caller asking about a claim status, a plan benefit,
  an enrollment change? That intent triggers parallel retrieval from the
  relevant knowledge bases — in WEX's case, the benefits plan documents,
  policy knowledge base, and the caller's account context from Salesforce.
  The results surface as a suggested response or next action in the agent
  desktop. The human agent decides to use it or not. The AI is a copilot,
  not a replacement. I have built this exact retrieval and classification
  architecture in the multi-agent service assistant I designed — the domain
  is different but the pattern is identical."

PART 4 — SALESFORCE SERVICE CLOUD (what you need to know)
---

You may not have 5+ years on Salesforce. Prepare the conceptual level.
Know enough to lead a team that builds on it, even if you did not build on it.

WHAT SERVICE CLOUD IS
  Salesforce's CRM product for customer service organizations.
  It is the system of record for:
    - Customer cases (service tickets, disputes, inquiries)
    - Case routing (who handles what type of case)
    - Knowledge base (articles agents use to answer questions)
    - Contact center integration (CTI — computer telephony integration)
    - Email, chat, and voice channel management
    - SLA tracking (service level agreements per case type)
    - Reporting and dashboards for call center managers

KEY COMPONENTS RELEVANT TO AGENTIC AI
  Salesforce Einstein: Salesforce's AI platform.
    Einstein Next Best Action: rule/ML-based recommendations for agents.
    Einstein Article Recommendations: suggests KB articles during a case.
    Einstein Conversation Mining: analyses call transcripts for trends.
    Einstein GPT (now Einstein Copilot): generative AI embedded in Service Cloud.
    These are Salesforce's native nudge capabilities. Your role is deciding
    which to use vs building custom agentic AI on top of the platform.

  Salesforce Flows: visual workflow automation within Salesforce.
    Used to automate case routing, approval processes, data updates.
    The existing workflow layer before AI augmentation.

  Salesforce Apex / LWC: code-level customization.
    Apex = server-side Java-like language.
    LWC (Lightning Web Components) = front-end components in the agent desktop.
    Your AI nudge widget lives here — a custom LWC that calls your agent API.

  OmniChannel: routes incoming cases, chats, and calls to available agents.
    Skills-based routing: match the case type to the right agent skill.
    AI can augment this: predict case type from initial description,
    route to the optimal agent before a human has read it.

  CTI (Computer Telephony Integration): connects phone system to Salesforce.
    When a call comes in, Salesforce pops the caller's record automatically.
    Your real-time nudge widget sits in this CTI context.

WHAT TO SAY WHEN ASKED ABOUT SALESFORCE DEPTH
  "My primary platform experience is cloud-native on AWS and GCP, not
  Salesforce. But I have led platform programs where I did not start with
  deep domain knowledge — the Harness IDP program is an example.
  What I bring to a Salesforce environment is: clear architectural thinking
  about where custom AI capability lives vs where you use native platform
  features (Einstein Copilot vs custom RAG agents), how to govern
  integrations between Salesforce and external AI services via APIs and
  event streams, and how to define SDLC and testing standards for
  AI-augmented Salesforce workflows. I would hire a strong Salesforce
  technical architect who owns the platform depth while I own the AI
  architecture and team delivery strategy."
  This is honest and senior. Do not pretend to know it. Show how you lead it.

---

"What is your experience with Salesforce Service Cloud?"

"How would you approach transforming a call center with agentic AI?"

  Phase 2: real-time intent classification + nudge suggestions.
  Phase 3: post-call automation (summarization, case creation, follow-up).
  Phase 4: inbound deflection (AI IVR, chat agent for simple cases).
  Phase 5: predictive (volume forecasting, churn signal detection).
  Each phase has measurable outcomes (AHT, FCR, CSAT, cost per contact).
  Each phase is delivered in a canary rollout, evaluated, then expanded.

"How do you manage a team of 60 across 4 global groups?"
  Specific answer:
  Structure: each team has an engineering manager I develop and trust.
  My role: set direction, remove blockers, review architecture,
  run a weekly leadership rhythm with my managers.
  Upskilling: structured learning program (not just self-study).
  AI literacy is not optional — every team member learns the fundamentals.
  I create on-ramps: lunch-and-learns, pair programming with AI tools,
  internal demos, documentation culture.
  Accountability: OKRs at the team level, visible to everyone.
  Team health: regular skip-levels, engagement check-ins, honest feedback.

"What does your AI governance framework look like for a call center?"
  Answer: same principles as your healthcare governance, new vocabulary.
  1. Human in the loop: agent suggestions never execute without rep action.
     The human presses the button. The AI suggests.
  2. Audit trail: every suggestion shown, every suggestion used or ignored,
     every call outcome. Immutable log. Compliance-ready.
  3. Explainability: every suggestion has a source citation.
     "This answer comes from the WEX HSA Eligible Expense Guide, page 4."
  4. Guardrails: content filtering on what the AI can suggest.
     Cannot suggest giving wrong regulatory information.
     Cannot suggest waiving fees without authorization.
  5. Evaluation: golden question set run against the KB weekly.
     If faithfulness score drops below threshold, alert the team.
     Model performance does not degrade silently.
  6. Model risk: new AI capabilities go through a validation process
     before touching live calls. Shadow mode first (AI generates suggestions
     but they are not shown to reps, only logged for evaluation).
     Validated -> canary (10% of calls) -> full rollout.

"How do you build a business case for AI investment?"
  Answer: use your ROI framework.
  Frame it in terms the business already measures:
  AHT, FCR, cost per contact, CSAT, compliance error rate, churn.
  "If nudge technology reduces AHT by 4% across 500 agents handling
  200 calls/day each: that is 500 * 200 * 5min * 0.04 = 20,000 minutes
  of agent time freed per day. At $X cost per minute of agent time,
  the annual savings are $Y. The AI platform investment is $Z.
  Payback period is Z/Y years. Beyond cost: FCR improvement reduces
  repeat calls, CSAT improvement reduces churn, compliance improvement
  reduces regulatory exposure."
  Always close with: "I validate these numbers with a pilot before
  committing to a full rollout. The pilot is how we earn the trust
  of the call center leadership."

"How do you handle the 'AI replacing jobs' concern with a call center team?"
  This will come up. WEX has 60 people in service roles.
  Answer: frame it correctly.
  "AI augments the best agents. The reps who use AI effectively handle
  more complex cases, deliver better outcomes, and have more satisfying
  work because they spend less time searching for answers and more time
  actually helping members. The goal is not to reduce headcount —
  it is to handle growing volume without proportional headcount growth,
  and to increase the quality and speed of service. I make this explicit
  with the team from day one. I bring reps into the design process.
  The people who will use the tool should help define what it surfaces.
  That buy-in is what makes adoption real."

"How do you prepare board-level AI materials?"
  Answer: executive communication is about outcomes and risk, not architecture.
  A board slide on AI strategy has three components:
  1. Where we are today (baseline — AHT, FCR, cost, CSAT).
  2. Where AI takes us (target state, timeline, investment required).
  3. What could go wrong and how we manage it (regulatory risk, adoption risk,
     technical risk, model performance risk).
  I work backwards from the decision the board needs to make.
  If they need to approve investment, the narrative is ROI and payback.
  If they need to approve risk appetite, the narrative is governance and controls.
  I use plain language, business metrics, and one architecture diagram max.
  I leave the technical depth for the appendix.
