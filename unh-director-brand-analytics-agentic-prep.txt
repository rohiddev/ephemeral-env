PART 2 — WHAT MMM IS (master this before the interview)
---

WHAT MARKETING MIX MODELING IS
   MMM is an econometric regression technique that decomposes historical
   sales or conversion outcomes into the contribution of each marketing
   channel and external factor.

   Example: UHC spends on TV, digital display, paid search, direct mail,
   and broker events. MMM tells you: of the 10,000 new E&I members
   acquired last quarter, how many were driven by each channel,
   by seasonality (open enrollment), by price changes, and by competitor
   actions — and what the ROI of each dollar was.

   The model output:
     Channel contribution curves (diminishing returns per dollar spent)
     ROI per channel (revenue or members per $1000 spent)
     Saturation points (at what spend level does a channel stop working?)
     Optimal budget allocation across channels to maximize acquisition

HOW MMM WORKS (simplified)
   Base variables: price, seasonality, economic conditions, competitor spend
   Marketing variables: TV GRPs, digital impressions, search spend, etc.
   Response transformation: adstock (how long does an ad's effect last?)
     and diminishing returns (hill function — each additional dollar buys less)
   Regression: fit a model that explains sales as a function of all variables
   Decomposition: attribute each unit of sales to each variable

   Modern MMM tools:
     Robyn (Meta, open source, R-based, uses Ridge regression + Nevergrad optimizer)
     Google Meridian (2024, open source, Bayesian MMM in Python)
     Analytic Partners (commercial, enterprise MMM vendor)
     Nielsen Marketing Cloud (legacy enterprise vendor)
     Neustar (TransUnion) (enterprise MMM)
     Recast (modern probabilistic MMM, startup)

   You need to know: Robyn and Meridian at the conceptual level.
   Know: what an adstock transformation does, what saturation means,
   what ROI curves look like, how budget optimization works.

KEY MMM CONCEPTS TO KNOW COLD

   Adstock (carryover effect)
     Advertising effect does not stop the day the ad runs.
     It decays over time. A TV ad this week still influences behavior
     next week and the week after, at a diminishing rate.
     Adstock transforms the raw spend variable to capture this decay.
     Parameter: half-life (how many weeks until 50% of the effect is gone).
     TV: long half-life (weeks). Digital: short half-life (days).

   Saturation / diminishing returns
     Each additional dollar of spend buys less incremental outcome.
     The first $100K on TV might drive 500 new members.
     The next $100K drives 300. The next $100K drives 100.
     The model fits a curve (hill function) to capture this.
     Implication for budget allocation: do not stack all spend in one channel
     past its saturation point. Spread across channels for efficiency.

   Incrementality
     The true causal lift from marketing, isolated from organic demand.
     Without incrementality: you credit marketing for members who would
     have joined anyway (during open enrollment).
     With incrementality: you measure only the lift above the baseline.
     MMM estimates incrementality via the base/media decomposition.
     Geo holdout tests (run the campaign in some markets, not others)
     are used to validate MMM incrementality estimates.

   Attribution vs MMM
     Attribution (last-touch, multi-touch, data-driven):
       Assigns credit for a conversion to touchpoints in a single user's journey.
       Works for digital only. Does not see offline channels.
       Biased toward bottom-funnel channels (paid search gets last touch).
     MMM:
       Aggregate model across all channels including offline.
       Measures macro-level effectiveness, not individual user journeys.
       Slower to update (weekly/monthly data). No user-level view.
     Best practice: use both. MMM for budget allocation strategy.
     Attribution for channel-level optimization and creative testing.

   Halo effect
     A marketing activity in one channel lifts performance in another.
     Example: a brand TV campaign increases branded paid search volume.
     More people search for "UnitedHealthcare plans" after seeing a TV ad.
     The TV campaign gets partial credit for the search conversion.
     Quantifying halo: MMM can model cross-channel interactions if
     designed to capture them (interaction terms, lagged effects).

   Brand health metrics
     Awareness: unaided (unprompted) vs aided (prompted after mention).
     Consideration: would you consider this brand for your next plan?
     Preference: which brand do you prefer among those you know?
     Net Promoter Score (NPS): would you recommend UHC to a friend?
     Purchase intent: how likely are you to choose this plan?
     Brand equity index: composite of the above.
     These are measured via survey panels (monthly or quarterly).
     MMM uses them as variables: does brand consideration drive
     incremental acquisition above what media alone explains?

   Budget optimization
     Given the fitted MMM and diminishing return curves:
     What is the optimal allocation of the total marketing budget
     across channels to maximize acquisitions (subject to constraints)?
     This is a constrained optimization problem:
     maximize: sum of channel contributions
     subject to: total spend = budget, each channel spend within bounds
     Solved with: Nevergrad (Robyn), scipy.optimize, or Bayesian optimization.
     The output is a recommended media plan.

OPEN ENROLLMENT CONTEXT FOR UHC
   UHC E&I sells health insurance to individuals and employer groups.
   Open enrollment (OE): November 1 – January 15 (ACA marketplace).
   Employer group renewal: year-round but peaks Q3-Q4.
   Marketing spend is heavily seasonal: 70%+ of annual budget in OE window.
   MMM must account for this: strong seasonal baseline during OE means
   marketing incremental lift is smaller (high organic demand already).
   Competitor actions during OE (BCBS, Cigna, Aetna) affect brand share.
   Regulatory changes (ACA subsidies, plan rules) are external drivers
   that MMM must include as variables.

PART 3 — THE AGENTIC AI LAYER (your strongest contribution)
---

This is where you own the room. The JD describes six agentic use cases.
Know each one with a specific architecture.

USE CASE 1 — FASTER ACCESS TO MMM OUTPUTS
   Problem: MMM results are delivered as Excel files, PowerPoint decks,
   or R/Python notebooks. Analysts spend hours finding the right number.
   "What was the TV ROI in Q3 last year?" requires opening 3 files.

   Agentic solution:
     MMM outputs (model coefficients, decomposition tables, ROI curves,
     budget scenarios) ingested into a structured knowledge base.
     RAG agent: analyst asks natural language question.
     Agent retrieves the relevant model output, formats the answer,
     cites the source model run and date.
     "TV ROI in Q3 2024 was $2.14 per $1 spent (Source: MMM_Q3_2024_v3.xlsx,
     Sheet: ROI Summary)."
   Your evidence: Bedrock Knowledge Bases + RAG agent pattern.
   Same architecture, different document type (MMM output instead of policy docs).

USE CASE 2 — EMERGING MARKET SIGNAL DETECTION
   Problem: brand perception shifts, competitor launches, regulatory changes
   happen in real time. The analyst team finds out weeks later via surveys.

   Agentic solution:
     Signal monitoring agent: polls daily —
       News feeds (healthcare industry, UHC brand mentions)
       Social listening (sentiment on UHC vs competitors)
       Regulatory filings (CMS rule changes, state insurance dept notices)
       Competitor pricing/plan changes (public data)
       Search trend data (Google Trends for plan-related queries)
     Classifier agent: scores each signal for relevance and urgency.
       "High urgency: Cigna launched a new $0 premium plan in Texas.
       Historical MMM shows competitor plan launches reduce UHC acquisition
       in affected markets by 8-12% for 6 weeks."
     Alert: surfaces to brand strategy team in Slack/email with context.
   Your evidence: intent classifier + supervisor pattern from multi-agent project.
   Same classification engine, different signal sources.

USE CASE 3 — SCALABLE AUTOMATED INSIGHT GENERATION
   Problem: MMM runs produce hundreds of outputs. An analyst manually
   writes the same narrative structure every cycle: here is performance,
   here is what changed, here are the recommendations.

   Agentic solution:
     Insight generation agent: receives new MMM run outputs (structured JSON
     or structured tables from Robyn/Meridian).
     Compares to prior run: what changed in ROI by channel?
     What moved toward/away from saturation?
     Which channels are underinvested vs overinvested vs prior?
     Generates a draft narrative in the standard reporting format.
     Human analyst reviews, edits, approves — does not write from scratch.
   Your evidence: aggregator.py + LLM summarization pattern.
   The agent structures the output. The human validates.

USE CASE 4 — CONTINUOUS OPTIMIZATION LOOPS
   Problem: media investment decisions happen quarterly during planning.
   Between planning cycles, the market changes but spend does not adapt.

   Agentic solution:
     Weekly optimization agent: ingests latest performance data
     (digital metrics, search volume, brand tracker pulse surveys).
     Runs against the current MMM response curves.
     Identifies: which channels are currently over/underperforming
     vs their MMM-predicted response?
     Recommends: tactical budget shifts within the approved plan
     (e.g., reallocate $200K from display to paid search this week).
     Flags for human approval if shift exceeds threshold.
     Approved shifts feed back into the performance tracking system.
   Your evidence: supervisor + human-in-the-loop gate pattern.
   Temporal for the weekly durable workflow.

USE CASE 5 — AGENTIC OPERATIONAL OPTIMIZATION (time and cost)
   Problem: the analytics team spends 60% of time on data prep, report
   generation, and answering the same questions repeatedly.

   Agentic solution:
     Data prep agent: automates ingestion, cleaning, and joining of
     marketing performance data from multiple sources (DV360, Google Ads,
     DCM, Salesforce Marketing Cloud, brand tracker vendor).
     Report generation agent: builds standard weekly/monthly decks
     automatically. Analyst reviews and customizes the exceptions.
     Q&A agent: answers recurring questions from stakeholders
     ("what was last week's cost per lead by channel?") without
     analyst involvement. Frees analysts for higher-order work.
   Your evidence: self-service workflow automation (IDP pattern applied
   to analytics operations instead of developer operations).

USE CASE 6 — REUSABLE ANALYTICS COMPONENTS
   Problem: every analyst builds their own version of the same analysis.
   Inconsistent methods, inconsistent numbers, conflicting reports.

   Agentic solution:
     Analytics library: standardized, version-controlled functions for
     common calculations (adstock transformation, ROI calculation,
     brand health composite score, funnel conversion rate).
     Agent-accessible: agents call these functions as tools via MCP.
     Governance: all published components are reviewed and approved.
     Audit trail: every calculation cites the function version used.
   Your evidence: tool architecture from multi-agent project.
   Tools are reusable, versioned, testable — the same principle.

"How would you design an agentic workflow to accelerate MMM insight delivery?"
   Use Use Case 3 from Part 3. Structured answer:
   Inputs: new MMM run output (structured table of contributions, ROIs, curves).
   Agent step 1: compare to prior run — delta analysis by channel.
   Agent step 2: classify changes (positive/negative, material/immaterial).
   Agent step 3: retrieve context (what market events explain the change?
   News signal agent: open enrollment started, competitor launched).
   Agent step 4: generate narrative draft (standard template filled in).
   Human review: analyst edits the draft, approves, publishes.
   Outcome: cycle time from model output to published insight drops
   from 3 days to 4 hours.

"What is the difference between MMM and multi-touch attribution, and when do you use each?"
   MMM: aggregate, all channels including offline, causal, slow to update,
   used for strategic budget allocation across channels.
   MTA: user-level, digital channels only, fast to update, biased toward
   last touch, used for tactical optimization within digital channels.
   Use both: MMM for the quarterly plan, MTA for weekly digital optimization.
   Triangulate: if MMM says TV drives 20% of acquisition but MTA says 0%
   (because TV is offline), trust MMM for TV, use MTA for digital.

"How do you validate that an MMM model is reliable before acting on it?"
   Four checks:
   1. Historical fit: does the model explain historical sales well?
      R-squared > 0.85 is a common threshold.
   2. Out-of-sample test: hold out the last 3 months. Does the model
      predict them accurately without seeing that data?
   3. Sanity checks: do the channel coefficients make business sense?
      TV should have a longer adstock than digital. High spend channels
      should have higher diminishing returns.
   4. Geo holdout: run a market test (media off in some markets).
      Does the MMM-predicted lift match the observed lift?
   If not: challenge the vendor's model assumptions. That is explicitly
   what the JD asks for ("challenging, validating, and enhancing
   vendor delivered models").

"How do you quantify brand halo effects?"
   Two approaches:
   1. In the MMM: add brand health metrics (aided awareness, consideration)
      as lagged variables explaining acquisition. Coefficient on brand
      consideration = how much each 1-point lift in consideration
      drives incremental acquisition, holding media spend constant.
   2. Cross-channel interaction terms: in the MMM, include an interaction
      term between TV spend and branded search volume. A significant
      positive interaction = TV drives search (halo). The coefficient
      quantifies the halo value.
   Key point: halo effects mean that the ROI of brand-building campaigns
   is underestimated if you only count direct response. The full ROI
   includes the lift to all other channels downstream.

"How do you manage a team of data scientists and analysts?"
   Technical bar: I set quality standards for model development —

   Development: I push analysts toward higher-order work by automating
   the routine (data prep, standard reports). Time saved on busywork
   = time available for insight generation.
   Career growth: clear paths from analyst to senior analyst to lead.
   Data scientists grow toward model ownership and methodology leadership.

"What is your experience with the healthcare payer landscape?"

PART 10 — RAPID-FIRE VOCABULARY
---

Adstock          Transformation of ad spend to capture carryover effect.
                 Geometric decay: effect_t = spend_t + decay * effect_(t-1)
                 Parameter: decay rate (0-1). Higher = longer lasting effect.

Saturation       Diminishing returns on spend. Modeled as hill function:
                 response = spend^alpha / (spend^alpha + gamma^alpha)
                 gamma = inflection point (spend at 50% max response)
                 alpha = shape (steepness of the curve)

Decomposition    Splitting total sales/acquisitions into components:
                 Base (organic) + each channel's contribution + seasonality.
                 Visualized as a stacked bar: each bar = one time period.

Budget optimizer Given response curves, find spend allocation that maximizes
                 total contribution subject to total budget constraint.
                 Robyn uses Nevergrad. Meridian uses NumPyro/JAX.

Incrementality   Causal lift above what would have happened without the
                 marketing activity. Measured via geo holdout, matched
                 market tests, or synthetic control methods.

Robyn            Meta's open-source MMM package. R-based. Uses Ridge
                 regression + Nevergrad for budget optimization.
                 Produces: response curves, budget allocator, one-pager.

Meridian         Google's open-source Bayesian MMM. Python-based. Uses
                 NumPyro (probabilistic programming). More uncertainty
                 quantification than Robyn. Newer (2024).

Brand tracker    Recurring survey panel measuring brand health metrics
                 (awareness, consideration, NPS) weekly or monthly.
                 Vendors: Kantar, Ipsos, YouGov, Morning Consult.

MLR              Medical Loss Ratio. ACA requirement that insurers spend
                 at least 80-85% of premium on medical care.
                 Limits marketing spend as a % of premium revenue.

ACA subsidies    Advanced Premium Tax Credits reducing plan cost for
                 individuals below 400% FPL. When subsidies increase
                 (Inflation Reduction Act extension), individual market
                 demand rises — organic baseline in MMM increases.

Halo effect      Lift to one channel from activity in another.
                 TV brand campaign increases branded paid search volume.
                 Quantified via cross-channel interaction terms in MMM.

ROAS             Return on Ad Spend = revenue / ad spend.
                 Simpler than ROI (no cost subtraction). Used in digital.

iROAS            Incremental ROAS — revenue that would not have happened
                 without the ad spend. The MMM measure. Always lower than ROAS.

MLflow           Open-source ML lifecycle platform. Experiment tracking,
                 model registry, model serving. Used in Databricks.
                 For MMM: track model runs, parameters, outputs, compare.

Delta Lake       Open-source storage layer on data lakes (S3, ADLS).
                 Adds ACID transactions, schema enforcement, time travel
                 to Parquet files. Used in Databricks for reliable data.

Unity Catalog    Databricks governance layer. Centralized metadata, access
                 control, lineage tracking across all Databricks assets.
                 Relevant for audit trail on analytics data and models.
