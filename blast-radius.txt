      ===
      How Dynatrace topology discovery works:

        Dynatrace OneAgent auto-discovers service-to-service calls and DB connections via SmartScape. You query it:

        GET /api/v2/topology/processes/{entityId}/relationships
        GET /api/v2/smartscape/services/{serviceId}

        For a service with dynatrace.com/entity-id: SERVICE-abc123 in its catalog annotation, this returns the actual upstream and downstream dependencies observed in production — including which
        databases it connects to, which services it calls, and which MQ endpoints it hits.

        ==
          What it catches:

          Dynatrace: loan-app → person-app (observed, real traffic)
          CMDB:      loan-app has no relationship to person-app
          Finding:   BLAST RADIUS INCOMPLETE — person-app not declared as dependency

          CMDB:      loan-app → legacy-auth-service
          Dynatrace: no traffic to legacy-auth-service in 90 days
          Finding:   STALE DECLARATION — relationship may be decommissioned

          ---
          Database-specific discovery:

          Dynatrace OneAgent detects DB connections automatically — no need to query the database itself. It shows:

          - Which service connects to which DB host
          - DB engine type (PostgreSQL, Oracle, etc.)
          - Query volume and latency

                 Datadog builds its topology from APM distributed traces rather than a network agent. It has three relevant APIs:

                 GET /api/v1/service_dependencies   — service-to-service calls (APM traces)
                 GET /api/v2/apm/services           — all instrumented services
                 Datadog Database Monitoring        — which services hit which databases

                 ==

                 1. How Does Blast Radius Get Populated?
                 Blast radius is not a single thing — it's a derived view assembled from multiple data sources. There are two surfaces where it appears in your architecture:
                 In the IDP Catalog (Harness IDP — Blast Radius Tab)
                 This is populated from catalog-info.yaml relationship declarations:
                 yaml# In catalog-info.yaml
                 spec:
                   dependsOn:
                     - component:payments-db
                     - component:payment-events-kafka
                     - component:partner-sftp
                 Harness IDP reads these declarations and builds the dependency graph automatically. No pipeline needed — it's a live query against the catalog entity graph every time a developer opens the tab.
                 What populates it:

                 spec.dependsOn — what this service consumes
                 spec.providesApis — what this service exposes
                 spec.consumesApis — what APIs this service calls
                 System membership (spec.system) — which platform owns this service

                 Limitation: Only as accurate as what teams have declared in catalog-info.yaml. This is exactly why drift detection matters — undeclared dependencies = invisible blast radius.
                 In ServiceNow (ITOM Service Map / Blast Radius)
                 This is populated from now.yaml relationship declarations via RapDev sync:
                 yaml# In now.yaml
                 dependencies:
                   databases:
                     - name: payments-db
                   kafka:
                     - topic: payment-events
                 RapDev syncs these to ServiceNow CMDB as CI relationship records (cmdb_rel_ci). ITOM Visibility then builds the service map from those relationships.
                 What populates it:

                 Every dependency declared in now.yaml becomes a CI relationship in CMDB
                 ITOM Visibility reads CI relationships to draw the service map
                 Blast radius = all CIs downstream of a given CI in that graph

                 3. What Happens to a Tier 1 Application?
                 This is the most important question — Tier 1 apps need special treatment because the blast radius consequences are greatest.
                 What "Tier 1" Means in Your Context
                 A Tier 1 application is your highest criticality service — typically:

                 Customer-facing or revenue-generating
                 Has the most downstream dependents
                 A P1 incident on a Tier 1 app triggers the highest severity response
                 Likely covered by regulatory/compliance requirements

                 How Tier 1 is Declared
                 In catalog-info.yaml:
                 yamlmetadata:
                   annotations:
                     tier: "1"
                 In now.yaml:
                 yamlservice:
                   tier: 1
                 Both files need to agree on tier. Inconsistency between them should itself be a drift flag.

                 Blast Radius Implications for Tier 1
                 If a Tier 1 app has an undeclared dependency (Tier 2 drift), the consequences are severe:
                 Tier 1 service: payments-api
                 Undeclared dependency: oracle-payments-db (not in now.yaml)

                 oracle-payments-db goes down
                       ↓
                 ITOM has no record of payments-api depending on it
                       ↓
                 Blast radius report: "No services impacted"
                       ↓
                 On-call team doesn't page payments-api team
                       ↓
                 Customer-facing payments fail
                       ↓
                 Manual investigation finds the missing dependency
                       ↓
                 30 minutes of avoidable downtime
                 This is the most compelling argument for enforcing drift resolution SLAs on Tier 1 — not 48 hours, but same business day.
                 Recommended Tier 1 Governance Rules

                 Tier 1 services cannot have open Tier 2 drift alerts for more than 4 hours during business hours
                 All Tier 1 suppressions require Platform Engineering co-approval — not just team lead
                 Tier 1 blast radius is reviewed monthly — even if no drift detected, confirm the dependency graph is current
                 Tier 1 services are seeded first in the 20k repo batch rollout — get the most critical services covered before lower tiers
                 Tier 1 catalog entries are mandatory — IDP scorecard gates: a Tier 1 service with incomplete catalog-info.yaml is an automatic escalation

                 The Tier 1 Batch Seeding Priority
                 When you run the batch program across 20k repos, don't do it alphabetically or randomly. Phase it by tier:
                 Phase 1: All Tier 1 services (highest risk, highest value)
                 Phase 2: Tier 2 services
                 Phase 3: Tier 3 and below
                 This means your ITOM service maps are most accurate for the services that matter most, earliest.
